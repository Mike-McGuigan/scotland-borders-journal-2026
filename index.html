The Family Map

Sharon's Borders

This trip begins long before the first diary entry. Sharon was born in Coldstream Cottage Hospital on May 6th, 1970, and the Borders have stayed quietly threaded through the family ever since. Coldstream, Kelso, Civic Week, Home Place, Blakelaw Farm, Uncle Jock and Auntie Jocelyn, the Kelso Colts, the Harlequins, and the cottage at Blakelaw all belong to that older map of memory.

Ellis and Murray

Ellis was excited to show Murray a piece of her own history. Not ancient history, exactly, though there would be plenty of that too, but family history: the places her mum came from, the roads she had travelled as a child, the town that gathered itself each year for Civic Week, and the farm cottage that had quietly become one of those fixed points in the family's memory.

The Wider Story

Murray had been to Scotland before, mostly to Edinburgh, and his mother is Scottish. This trip would be a quieter doorway into the place: fields, river towns, old roads, horses, farms, abbey stones, and the Borders unfolding at their own pace. Cameron had been here before too, including last year. Sophie has not been to this part with us yet, though she has her own Scottish roots in Glasgow and Bridge of Weir.

Coldstream Civic Week

Where the Town Becomes a Living Memory

Coldstream Civic Week is the town's annual celebration of history, heritage, community, and Border tradition. Held in the first full week of August, it belongs to the wider family of Scottish Borders common ridings and festivals, with ride-outs, ceremonies, parades, fancy dress, and town events running through the week.

The presence of the Coldstream Guards connects the modern celebration back to the town's wider military history: Coldstream is the place that gave the regiment its name, and during Civic Week that connection becomes visible in the streets, ceremonies, photographs, and memories of the week.

At the centre of it all is the Thursday Flodden Ride, when the Coldstreamer and hundreds of riders make their way from Coldstream across the Tweed and out to Branxton Hill, commemorating the Battle of Flodden in 1513. The day carries both spectacle and solemnity: horses, standards, pipe music, crowds, wreath-laying, remembrance, and the distinctive presence of the Guards alongside the town's own traditions.
